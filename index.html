Not sure what a word means? Tap here for a plain-English glossary
Kubernetes ("K8s")
Runs and looks after many small programs at once, restarting or scaling them automatically.
Cluster
A group of computers working together as one system, managed by Kubernetes.
Terraform / IaC
Writing code that builds cloud infrastructure automatically, instead of clicking around by hand.
AWS / Azure / GCP
The three biggest cloud computing companies — Amazon, Microsoft, Google.
DevOps
One team both builds the software and keeps it running day to day.
Platform Engineering
Building tools that let other engineers run their own software without hand-holding.
Cloud Engineering
Focused on the infrastructure itself — networks, storage, accounts.
GitOps
The "master plan" lives in code; tools apply it automatically.
ArgoCD / Helm
Popular tools that auto-install and update software on Kubernetes.
Crossplane
Manages cloud infrastructure the same way Kubernetes manages software.
Self-service platform
Lets engineers get what they need on their own, without a specialist.
CI/CD
Automated pipelines that test and release code changes.
RBAC
The rules for who is allowed to do what inside a system.
Bootcamp transitioner
Moved into tech via a short course, not years of hands-on work.
"Buzzword" CV
Impressive-sounding tools and claims, with little real detail behind them.
Ops vs. platform
Ops keeps existing systems running (tickets, monitoring, admin). Platform work builds something other engineering teams use on their own. The same tools can be either — it's the career story that tells them apart.

STEP 02

Technical signals

The hands-on depth behind the buzzwords.

Kubernetes experience
Driver vs. mechanic — deploying it vs. running it.
What's Kubernetes?

Software that runs and looks after many programs at once. A "cluster" is the group of computers it manages.

Does their career read as ops, or as platform?
This can override raw tool depth — read it carefully.
Why does this matter so much?

Even genuine, hands-on Kubernetes work can still read as "ops" if the surrounding career is general infrastructure admin (tickets, monitoring, on-call support) rather than building something other engineering teams use on their own. Hiring panels have declined technically competent CVs on this basis alone.

STEP 03

Background & CV read

The mindset question below matters more than the entire technical checklist.

Do they show real investigative curiosity?
The single biggest factor in most platform-team hiring decisions — bigger than any tool on the CV.
What does "real curiosity" look like?

When something breaks or is unfamiliar, do they dig in — read the docs or source, test a hypothesis, tinker at home — rather than take the easiest path and stop? A junior with no production experience but this trait is worth more than a senior without it. Look for home-lab tinkering, researching a technology unprompted, or explaining why they chose an approach, not just that it worked.
